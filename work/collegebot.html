01 / Empathy — Understanding students

Context & Customer Research

The first question was where a campus assistant could reduce the effort of finding useful help.

I synthesized behavioral data, 1,200+ survey responses, and 40+ interviews across 18 colleges to understand where students struggled. Four recurring needs shaped the product direction: a clear starting point, school-relevant answers, easier resource discovery, and confidence in using AI.

1,200+survey responses
40+interviews
18colleges
A clearer starting point+

New students felt overwhelmed by unfamiliar resources and did not know where to begin. I prioritized guided onboarding and a clearer first-search entry to help them take one useful action.

School-relevant answers+

Students needed guidance that matched their university, major, and courses. This made visible school context and university-specific resource discovery central to the experience.

Less fragmented discovery+

Academic planning and career resources were spread across separate tools and channels. I focused the core experience on finding relevant help before expanding the number of features.

Confidence in using AI+

Classmate search through the school-specific chatbot raised a privacy concern. I researched how other products explained privacy, co-developed a questionnaire with four teammates, and synthesized the findings into a Privacy Q&A.

Market context

Students already had access to advisors, planning tools, job boards, and online communities. The opportunity was to bring relevant help into one experience organized around their school, course, and immediate task.

03 / Ideation — Exploring and choosing solutions

Brainstorming & Potential Solutions

I compared solutions against each group’s immediate needs, the value they could create, and the dependencies they would add. The priority was to make the first useful action clear before expanding into advanced academic and career tools.

Help a new student begin

Student need
Orientation, relevant guidance, and less information overload.
Candidate solutions
Step-by-step onboarding, school-specific resources, and AI recommendations.
Trade-offs
Onboarding needs content and design coordination. Campus resources depend on coverage and freshness. Personalization adds data and model dependencies.
Proposed scope
Start with a clear entry and minimum useful school context. Evaluate personalization after the first task works.

Support deeper academic work

Student need
Advanced study, relevant research opportunities, and time management.
Candidate solutions
Study tools, an opportunity finder, and an integrated calendar.
Trade-offs
Breadth can add value but expands content coverage, integrations, and maintenance.
Proposed scope
Explore one well-defined academic task after validating resource relevance.

Prepare students for work

Student need
Career direction, tailored search, and interview readiness.
Candidate solutions
Career toolkit, industry-filtered job search, and virtual coaching.
Trade-offs
Job data freshness and career guidance quality introduce external dependencies and trust risks.
Proposed scope
Keep career discovery visible. Validate advanced coaching and job integrations before committing delivery scope.

03 / Ideation — Making the first action tangible

Prototype 1.0

The first version let students choose their university, ask campus questions, or search for jobs. Suggested questions helped them get started, while the sidebar kept saved jobs and search history accessible.

03 / Ideation — Making the first action tangible

Prototype 2.0

Research into comparable products and experimentation informed our UI/UX work. Working with Engineering and Design, I helped move the campus-specific GPT toward a cleaner white interface with General and Class-specific experiences, simpler navigation, and relevant, high-traffic campus blog posts on the homepage.

  1. University context

    Keep the school visible while asking a question. Test whether students notice and correctly understand that context.

  2. General / Class and question box

    Make the first task prominent. Test whether the switch and prompt help students choose an entry and complete a first search.

  3. Campus content and simpler navigation

    Surface relevant, high-traffic campus blog posts alongside example questions, with fewer sidebar destinations to navigate.

05 / Validation — Results and learning

Validation, Outcomes & Learning

I worked with Engineering and Design on onboarding/search experimentation, standardized feature-launch evaluation, and established weekly product performance reviews.

Onboarding & Search

25% lowerbounce / early exit
40% higherfirst-time feature adoption

I owned onboarding and search experimentation with Engineering and Design. The reported results cover the broader Prototype 1.0-to-2.0 iteration, separately from the logo-placement test.

Bounce / early exit
The share of site visitors who viewed the page and left without exploring any feature decreased by 25%.
First-time feature adoption
The share of active accounts that used the feature was 40% higher for the later General and Class-specific GPT experience than for the initial campus-specific GPT.

I defined metrics across activation, search success, CTR, adoption, and retention to evaluate feature launches and guide weekly product reviews. The comparison does not isolate one UI change’s effect.

Trust & Privacy Q&A

80% fewerprivacy tickets in the reported comparison period
32% higherobserved first-week retention versus the pre-Q&A period

After users could search for classmates through the school-specific chatbot, I raised the privacy concern and researched how other products communicated privacy. I co-developed a questionnaire with four teammates, helped distribute it across schools, and synthesized the findings into the Privacy Q&A.

Ticket comparisons
Privacy-related support tickets were 80% lower in the applicable comparison period. A separate, later observation window recorded no privacy tickets.
Retention definition
The percentage of new users who opened the platform more than three times within their first week was 32% higher after publication than in the pre-Q&A period.

The retention result is an observed before-and-after change, not an isolated estimate of the Q&A’s effect.

Growth

10K+active users by my departure in December 2024
75% belowplanned promotion budget

Campus flyers paired invite codes with incentives. We followed with viral TikTok videos and a small paid promotion. The campaign performed above its KPI targets, while actual promotion spend was 75% below the planned budget.

One-day turnaround on reported UI details

After launch, I created a shared issue-tracking table with screenshots, issue locations, short descriptions, owners, and status updates. It reduced back-and-forth with Engineering and Design and helped the team resolve the reported UI details in one day.

What I would validate next

The next decision is whether first-search success predicts useful repeat behavior across campuses and acquisition channels. I would first inspect that relationship by cohort, then evaluate a focused onboarding/search change with agreed eligibility, success criteria, guardrails, and an observation window.

A future experiment would use activation and first-search success as early signals, retention as a longer-term signal, and privacy-related support demand as a guardrail. Sample-size planning and decision thresholds would follow baseline analysis.

06 / Business viability — Sustaining useful value

Business Model

I considered how CollegeBot could sustain useful academic and career support. Students are the primary users, while universities and advisors represent a potential institutional customer group with different purchasing and integration needs.

Student value

Users
College students with orientation, study, and career needs.
Value
Relevant resources and guidance in the context of their university.
Potential payment
A free entry with a proposed premium subscription for deeper tools and support.

Institution value

Potential customers
Universities, academic institutions, and career advisors.
Value
Shared access, tailored onboarding, and potential system integration.
Potential payment
Institutional licensing based on size and needs.

I tested acquisition across TikTok, Meta, SEO, and campus activations. Content, tutorials, and college communities offered additional ways to help students discover the platform. Beyond subscriptions and institutional licensing, advertising and commission sharing were potential revenue options to evaluate.

The main cost considerations were development, staffing, infrastructure, data, support, moderation, and marketing. I would compare those costs with repeated student value before committing to a paid offering.

06 / Business viability — Connecting the assumptions

Lean Canvas

The canvas connects the student problem with the product’s value, delivery costs, acquisition channels, and potential revenue. It provides a compact view of the business assumptions that would need validation.

Problem

Difficult resource discovery, generic academic/career guidance, and stress around the next academic or career step. Existing alternatives include advisors, calendars, job boards, and educational forums.

Customer Segments

Students are primary users. Freshman Explorers are the initial design focus. Institutions and career advisors are potential secondary customers.

Unique Value Proposition

School-relevant academic and career support, with an understandable path from the first question to useful resources.

Solution

Onboarding and university-specific discovery first, with planning, career tools, and community as broader candidate scope.

Channels

TikTok, Meta, SEO, and campus activations, supported by content, tutorials, and college communities. Web and mobile access are potential distribution routes.

Revenue Streams

Potential student subscriptions and institutional licensing, with advertising and career-platform commission sharing as additional options to evaluate.

Cost Structure

Development, staff, infrastructure, third-party data, support/moderation, marketing, and potential integration costs.

Key Metrics

Activation, search success, CTR, adoption, and retention, alongside potential business measures such as DAU/MAU, free-to-paid conversion, NPS, and CSAT.

Unfair Advantage

School-specific context, tailored guidance, and deeper resource integrations could differentiate the experience as content coverage and usefulness improve.
