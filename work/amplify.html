01 / Market entry & long-term planning

Building a Repeatable Roadmap for Florida ELA Adoption

State-level ELA adoption planning runs across a long horizon and multiple teams. The challenge was to decide whether Amplify should enter Florida ELA adoption and translate that decision into a plan that connected product development with market preparation.

Decision
Should Amplify enter Florida ELA adoption, and what research, timing, and readiness would support the entry plan?
My Role
Owned adoption planning and market-entry analysis; created a shared directory and reusable roadmap template; aligned Sales, Marketing, and Analytics on research, timelines, and launch gates.
Evidence
Lessons from prior adoption cycles, interviews with two experienced colleagues, and research and product-readiness planning across a 2026–2031 horizon.
Outcome
A shared directory and reusable roadmap designed to reduce future planning effort by approximately 50%. This is an expected benefit, not a measured time saving.

Recommendation: enter Florida ELA adoption

Based on the market-entry analysis, I recommended entering Florida ELA adoption. The recommendation was supported by a structured planning approach and a shared view of the research and readiness work ahead.

From adoption learning to an operating plan

Learn from past cycles

I reviewed prior adoption learning and interviewed two colleagues with relevant experience to identify the questions the plan needed to address.

Make the work reusable

Earlier Florida planning information was spread across people and documents, requiring repeated follow-up. I consolidated it into a shared directory and created a reusable adoption-planning roadmap template.

Align across functions

I aligned Sales, Marketing, and Analytics around research responsibilities, timelines, and launch gates so the work could be coordinated across teams.

Reflection. This work required both a market-entry judgment and a long-term plan others could use. Making the work repeatable helped turn a complex strategy question into coordinated action across functions.

02 / Competitive research & positioning

Finding the Differentiation Opportunity in ELA

I compared Amplify CKLA 3e with McGraw Hill Emerge! to help the team understand differentiation opportunities and potential competitive threats. My contribution centered on product evidence and the questions it raised for positioning.

Decision
Where can CKLA 3e differentiate, and what competitive gaps or threats should the team understand?
My Role
Researched and compared product components, reviewed product-facing materials and teacher/student experiences, and contributed to the differentiation section of a competitive deep-dive presentation.
Evidence
A product-level comparison of Amplify CKLA 3e and McGraw Hill Emerge!, organized around instructional components, teacher and student experiences, and digital resources.
Outcome
Clarified product-level points of differentiation and contributed evidence to the team’s competitive discussion.

Comparison dimension → Evidence reviewed → Strategic question

Instructional components

Evidence reviewed
How different materials interpret the same standards.
Strategic question
How do the products present their instructional choices?

Teacher experience

Evidence reviewed
Teacher-facing materials and the instructional choices they present.
Strategic question
What support do the materials give educators when making instructional decisions?

Student experience

Evidence reviewed
Student-facing materials, including support for multilingual learners.
Strategic question
How is support for different learning needs presented?

Digital resources

Evidence reviewed
Digital resource overviews and their place in the product experience.
Strategic question
Where do digital resources create a competitive opportunity or raise a question to investigate?

Reflection. Although I had not taught in a classroom, I learned to examine the materials from both an educator’s perspective and a product reviewer’s perspective. Reviewing standards, instructional choices, and multilingual-learner support helped me frame useful questions and contribute my evaluation to the competitive deep-dive presentation.

03 / Product analytics & prioritization

Finding Where Teachers Got Stuck

I analyzed 400K+ teacher sessions from Q1–Q2 2026 in SQL and Looker to map how teachers navigated the product to reach a textbook. The analysis identified five recurring routes and three high-friction navigation points for the product team to review.

Decision
Which teacher navigation problems should the product team prioritize?
My Role
Built a SQL and Looker dashboard to map routes to textbook access and identify high-friction navigation points.
Evidence
400K+ teacher sessions in Q1–Q2 2026; five recurring navigation routes; three high-friction navigation points; a 15-page insights deck.
Outcome
Findings informed UX and roadmap recommendations.

From session behavior to a product discussion

  2. 5

In a generalized example, a teacher could open the same textbook from a file entry point or reach it through another page. Comparing these routes helped me identify where navigation needed closer review.

I brought the recurring routes and high-friction navigation points together in a 15-page insights deck. The findings informed UX and roadmap recommendations.

Reflection. The contribution was to make navigation problems concrete and discussable. The analysis supported prioritization; the next step would be to evaluate any implemented changes against clearly defined product measures.

Across these projects, I turned teacher behavior, product comparisons, and market learning into clearer decisions about what to prioritize, where to compete, and how to prepare teams for the next step.
